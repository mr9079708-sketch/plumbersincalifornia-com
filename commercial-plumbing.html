During your visit

What happens during your visit

Here is what happens when our technician visits, in order:

  1. Initial consultation: the visit starts with an assessment of your plumbing needs. The technician or project lead talks through what is happening, which fixtures and systems are affected, and how the business uses its plumbing day to day. A restaurant kitchen, an office block and a retail shop put different demands on their plumbing, and the plan has to fit the business, not just the pipes.
  2. Detailed inspection: a licensed California commercial plumber conducts a thorough inspection of the systems involved, including the fixtures, the drains, the water heater and the visible pipework. The inspection finds the actual cause and checks whether the problem is local to one fixture or part of a wider issue in the building's plumbing.
  3. Customised proposal: after the inspection, you receive a detailed proposal outlining the necessary services, costs and timelines. For commercial work this is a proper proposal, not a verbal figure: it says what will be done, what materials are included, how long it will take, and what it will cost, so you can plan around it and budget for it properly.
  4. Scheduling around your business: once the proposal is approved, the work is scheduled to cause minimal disruption. That often means working outside business hours, in stages, or in the quietest part of the day. A restaurant cannot lose its kitchen during service, and an office cannot lose its restrooms during working hours, so the schedule is agreed with you before anything starts.
  5. Implementation: the team performs the required services using quality materials, working to the proposal and keeping you informed as the work progresses. If the inspection uncovers something the proposal did not cover, it is raised with you and agreed before any extra work is done.
  6. Testing every fixture and system: when the work is done, every fixture and system that was touched is tested: taps run, drains are checked for flow, toilets flush properly, and the water heater delivers hot water as it should. Commercial plumbing serves many people at once, so testing is done across the premises, not just at one tap.
  7. Follow-up: a follow-up visit checks that the plumbing systems are still functioning optimally after the work has had time to settle. If anything is not right, it is addressed then. The follow-up is also the point at which a maintenance routine can be agreed, so the same problem does not come back.
Typical scenarios

Typical commercial plumbing problems we are called for

Business plumbing fails differently from home plumbing: more people use it, it runs for longer hours, and when it stops, the business stops with it. These are the patterns we see most often.

A restaurant kitchen drain backing up during service

A blocked kitchen drain can close a restaurant. Grease, food waste and heavy daily use build up in commercial kitchen drains faster than in a home, and when the drain backs up, the kitchen cannot operate. The work starts with clearing the drain and checking the whole kitchen drainage run, not just the one sink that is slow, and it is usually scheduled so the kitchen loses as little service time as possible. Regular drain cleaning is the prevention that keeps this from happening again.

Office restrooms out of order

Running toilets, leaking taps and blocked restroom drains are the everyday commercial calls. One running toilet in an office wastes water all day, and a restroom that is out of order affects every member of staff. The inspection covers all the restroom fixtures, because a problem in one is often a sign of wear across all of them, and the proposal will say whether a single repair or a wider replacement of worn parts is the better answer.

A water bill that has risen sharply

A business that sees its water bill rise without any change in use usually has a leak somewhere, often a running toilet, a leaking tap that is never turned fully off, or a hidden leak in the pipework. The visit starts with checking the fixtures and the meter, then the pipework, and the repair is quoted on what is actually found. Finding the leak early matters, because a business leak wastes water every hour the premises are open.
