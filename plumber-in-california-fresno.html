What your home’s age means

What Fresno’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in Fresno all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in Fresno usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Before 1960, the standard was galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both are now past their best in Fresno. The steel rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure falls and the water runs brown; the cast iron scales and sags until drains block more often. Test the lines before anything fails — a pressure and flow test, plus a camera look at the drains, tells you what is urgent and what can wait.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Fresno. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

PEX and CPVC define homes built from the 1990s onward: flexible pipe that resists freezing and scale, and installs quickly. The weak points moved, they did not vanish — fittings, manifolds and the quality of the installation now decide whether the system is trouble-free. Exposed runs still need protection from sun, and a bad manifold will show up as pressure problems in one part of the house and not another.

In the Valley, hard water is the thread running through all three eras: it scales galvanised lines, clogs copper fittings and settles in PEX manifolds alike. Whatever the pipe material, a water heater that is flushed annually and an anode rod that is replaced on schedule will outlast one that is never serviced.

Fresno through the year

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Fresno

Fresno's weather shapes its plumbing across the year. The notes below cover what each season usually brings — and what to check before it arrives.

Spring

Spring is when winter's damage shows up: gutters full of debris, drains that slowed over the cold months, and the first heavy rain testing all of it at once.

Tip: Clear the gutters and check the drains before the spring rain arrives — a slow drain in March is a backup in April.

Summer

Summer is the season for outdoor plumbing: hoses, irrigation and the lines that sit in the sun all get used hard, and heat expands every joint in the system.

Tip: Check outdoor taps and hose connections in early summer, and watch the water bill — a rise with no change in use usually means a leak somewhere.

Fall

Fall is a time for preparing plumbing for winter, including checking for leaks and insulating pipes.

Tip: Consider scheduling a maintenance check to ensure your plumbing is ready for cooler temperatures.

Winter

Winter is when the cold tests the plumbing: a cold snap can freeze an exposed pipe, a scaled heater gives up under the extra load, and the first storm tests every drain.

Water and weather in Fresno

Water and weather in Fresno: what it does to plumbing

Plumbing fails for local reasons. In Fresno the local reasons are hard water and long, hot summers, plus very hot, dry summers and cool, foggy winters — and understanding them is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that comes back next season.

Water is the defining plumbing factor in Fresno. Supply across the Central Valley is hard, and mineral scale is the result: it narrows pipes, clogs aerators and showerheads, and settles inside water heaters, where it insulates the burner or element from the water. A heater full of scale works harder, costs more to run and dies years early. Flushing the tank once a year and replacing the anode rod on schedule is the cheapest maintenance in plumbing, and in Fresno County it is the maintenance that matters most.

Heat is the Valley's acute problem. Long summers expand exposed supply lines, bake hoses and irrigation connections, and crack the plastic fittings left in direct sun. The failures cluster in August, and they are found as damp patches by the house wall or bills that climb for no reason. Shading or lagging exposed runs before the heat peaks takes an hour.

The Valley's other signature is dust. Dry-season dust and field debris find their way into outdoor drains and gutters, and sit there until the first autumn rain washes them into a solid blockage. A clear-out before the first rain is good practice, and it is the cheapest drainage job of the year. The same dust gets into irrigation filters and outdoor tap aerators, so a weak outdoor tap in September is usually a clogged filter, not a supply problem.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common in the Central Valley

Valley plumbing lives with hard water and hard summers. These are the faults that fill our diary in Fresno.

  • High summer demand — garden watering and long showers push older systems hard, and weak points tend to fail in July and August.
  • Water heater sediment — mineral sediment settles at the bottom of the tank, making the heater work harder, sound louder and die younger.
  • Furred-up taps and showerheads — white scale on fixtures is the visible half of the problem; the same scale is narrowing the pipes behind the wall.
  • Split outdoor fittings — heat expansion and UV wear crack exposed plastic and rubber fittings, usually discovered as a puddle by the house wall.
  • Dust and debris in outdoor drains — dry-season dust and field debris collect in gullies, then the first autumn rain turns them into a blockage.

Flushing the water heater once a year and checking outdoor lines before summer are the two cheapest habits in Valley plumbing.
