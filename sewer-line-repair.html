About this service

Understanding sewer line repair services in California

A sewer line carries waste water away from the home to the street, and a problem in it is a problem for the whole home, not one fixture. Repairing it means diagnosing first, with a camera, because the line cannot be seen, and then fixing the actual damage, whether that is a clog, tree roots, a cracked section or a collapsed pipe.

Tree roots seek out the water in a sewer line and grow into it; older pipes corrode and crack; heavy rain can overload a line that is already weak. Every sewer job starts with a camera inspection, because the repair plan has to be based on what is actually down there, not on a guess.

  • ✓ Prevent costly damage to the whole home
  • ✓ Improve plumbing efficiency from the main line out
  • ✓ Increase property value with a sound sewer line
  • ✓ Reduce unpleasant odors at the source

California adds its own difficulties to sewer lines. Older homes often have clay or cast iron pipes that have been in the ground for decades, and they corrode from the inside until they leak or collapse. Trees grow roots into lines looking for water, and ground movement can crack a pipe that was sound for years. That is why every sewer job starts with a camera inspection: the line cannot be seen, the damage cannot be guessed at, and the repair plan has to be based on what is actually down there. A sewer line repaired on a guess is a sewer line that will need repairing again.

During your visit

What happens during your visit

Here is what happens when our technician visits, in order:

  1. Initial camera inspection: the visit starts with a thorough inspection of your sewer line using camera technology. The camera is fed into the line from a clean-out or an access point, and the technician watches the inside of the pipe on a screen. The initial camera inspection typically takes 30-60 minutes. This is the step that makes sewer repair different from drain cleaning: the diagnosis is based on what is actually down there, not on a guess.
  2. Finding the problem: the camera shows exactly where the damage is and what kind it is: tree roots growing into the line, a cracked or corroded section, a collapsed pipe, or a blockage that drain cleaning cannot clear. The technician notes the location and the extent of the damage, because both decide what the repair will involve.
  3. Diagnosis and options: after the inspection, the technician diagnoses the problem and discusses the best repair options with you. A small section of damaged pipe is a different job from a long run of collapsed line, and a line that is easy to reach is a different job from one buried under a driveway. The options are explained in plain terms, with what each one involves.
  4. Repair planning: a detailed repair plan is developed for your specific needs, including timelines, costs and materials. The plan says which section will be repaired or replaced, what materials will be used, such as PVC pipes and sealants, how long the work will take, and what it will cost. Most repairs are completed within a day, minimising disruption to your home.
  5. Execution of repairs: the skilled technicians perform the necessary repairs using high-quality materials. The damaged section is repaired or replaced, the joints are sealed, and the work is done so the line is not only fixed but will last. Where digging is needed, the work area is kept as small as the repair allows.
  6. Final camera check and flow test: after the repair, the camera is run through the line again to confirm the repair is sound and the pipe is clear, and water is run through the drains to check the flow. A sewer repair is not finished on the word of the person who did it; it is finished when the camera shows a clear, sound pipe and the drains run freely.
  7. Final inspection and cleanup: a final inspection confirms everything is functioning correctly and meets local codes, and the work area is cleaned up. Cleanup is included in the service, so your home and garden are left tidy, not dug up and abandoned.
  8. Walkthrough and what to watch for: the technician explains what was found, what was repaired, and what to watch for afterwards: drains that run slowly across the whole house, foul smells, or sewage backing up are the signs that the main line needs attention again. The technician will also advise on what should not go down the drains, because grease, wipes and other waste are what block a sewer line in the first place.

Tree roots in the line

Tree roots grow into sewer lines looking for water, and once they are in, they catch everything that passes and grow into a blockage. The signs are gradual: drains that get slower across the whole house, gurgling, and eventually a backup. The camera inspection shows exactly where the roots are and how much of the line they have affected, and the repair plan is based on that, not on a guess. Clearing the roots alone is not enough if the pipe is cracked where they got in, and the plan will say so.

Repeated backups in the lowest bathroom

When sewage backs up into the lowest bathroom or the lowest fixture in the home, the blockage is usually in the main line, not in the fixture itself. A drain cleaning may clear it for a while, but if it comes back, the cause is in the sewer line: a damaged section, a collapsed pipe, or roots. The camera inspection finds the cause, and the repair fixes the line itself, which is what stops the backups coming back.
