What your home’s age means

What Fontana’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Fontana can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

A pre-1960 home in Fontana usually runs on galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains, and both wear from the inside out. The supply lines rust and narrow, so pressure falls and water runs brown after the house has stood empty; the drains scale and sag, so they block more often and clear more slowly. The work on a home of this age is usually staged: test the lines, replace the worst run first, and plan the rest over a year or two rather than all at once.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

Homes from the 1960s to the 1980s usually have copper supply lines and ABS or PVC drains — a big step up. Copper lasts well, but it can develop pinhole leaks where the water is aggressive or the pipe was poorly installed, and the drains from this era are now reaching the age where joints and fittings start to fail. The work here is usually targeted: replace the worst run, not the whole system.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

From the 1990s onward, PEX and CPVC took over: flexible, freeze-resistant, quick to install. The failure points moved to the fittings and the manifold, and to exposed runs left in the sun. A home of this era in Fontana rarely needs repiping — it needs its fittings, its manifold and its water heater checked, which is a smaller job and a shorter visit.

Across inland California, the habits matter more than the era: flush the heater annually, replace the anode rod on schedule, descale the aerators when flow drops, and check outdoor lines before the heat peaks. Whatever the pipes are made of, those four jobs are what keep a system healthy.

Plumbing problems common across California

Across California, the same five plumbing problems turn up in town after town — including Fontana.

  • Ageing water heaters — a tank heater usually lasts around a decade, and an old unit can fail suddenly, flooding the space around it.
  • Hard water scale — mineral buildup shortens water heater life and clogs fixtures in most of the state.
  • Ageing pipes in older homes — galvanised and cast-iron pipework corrodes from the inside, cutting pressure and tinting water.
  • Tree roots in sewer lines — roots find joints in old clay and cast-iron laterals, and slow drains across the home are the warning sign.
