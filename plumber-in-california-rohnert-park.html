Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common in the Bay Area

Bay Area plumbing has a character of its own: old pipes, hills, big trees and earthquakes. These are the problems that come up again and again in Rohnert Park.

  • Old water heaters in small spaces — Bay Area utility closets and garages often hold ageing heaters that fail without warning, and access for replacement is tight.
  • Ageing galvanised pipes — many homes still have original steel supply lines that corrode from the inside, cutting pressure and tinting the water brown.
  • Tree roots in sewer laterals — mature street trees send roots into old clay and cast-iron joints, and the first sign is slow drains in several rooms at once.
  • Hillside drain runs — long laterals on sloped lots are harder to clear and more prone to sagging sections where waste collects.
  • Seismic vulnerability — rigid gas and water connections can fail in a quake, which is why flexible connectors and shut-off valves are standard precautions here.

A camera inspection of the sewer line and a look at the age of the supply pipes answers most Bay Area plumbing questions before they become emergencies.
