What your home’s age means

What Daly City’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Daly City can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Before 1960, the standard was galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both are now past their best in Daly City. The steel rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure falls and the water runs brown; the cast iron scales and sags until drains block more often. Test the lines before anything fails — a pressure and flow test, plus a camera look at the drains, tells you what is urgent and what can wait.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Daly City. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

Homes built from the 1990s onward usually have PEX or CPVC supply lines — flexible, resistant to freezing and scale, and quick to install. They are not trouble-free: fittings matter, ultraviolet light degrades exposed runs, and a bad manifold installation causes pressure problems. The checks here are different — fittings, manifolds and the water heater — rather than the pipes themselves.

In the Bay Area, the ground is the factor no era escapes: clay soils swell and shrink with the seasons, working joints open and bellying buried laterals. Whatever the supply pipes are made of, a camera inspection of the lateral is worth doing on any older home here.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common in the Bay Area

Bay Area plumbing has a character of its own: old pipes, hills, big trees and earthquakes. These are the problems that come up again and again in Daly City.

  • Seismic vulnerability — rigid gas and water connections can fail in a quake, which is why flexible connectors and shut-off valves are standard precautions here.
  • Damp winters and fog — cool, moist air keeps crawl spaces damp for months, hiding slow leaks and corroding exposed fittings.
  • Discoloured water from old pipes — rust inside ageing supply lines shows up first as brown water from a tap that has stood overnight.
  • Root intrusion in old sewers — clay and cast-iron laterals under mature trees are the usual route for roots, and they return unless the joint is repaired.
  • Low pressure at upstairs taps — long runs and narrow, scaled pipes struggle to feed upper floors, especially in hillside homes.

Start with the age of the pipes and the state of the sewer line — those two checks tell you most of what you need to know.
