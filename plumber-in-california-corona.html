What your home’s age means

What Corona’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Corona can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Homes built before about 1960 usually have galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains. Galvanised steel rusts from the inside, so the bore narrows over decades: pressure falls, water runs brown after a holiday away, and eventually a pinhole leak appears. Cast-iron drains scale and sag in the same way. A home of this age in Corona is worth a proper look at the pipework, even if nothing has failed yet — the first signs are easy to miss.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Corona. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

Homes built from the 1990s onward usually have PEX or CPVC supply lines — flexible, resistant to freezing and scale, and quick to install. They are not trouble-free: fittings matter, ultraviolet light degrades exposed runs, and a bad manifold installation causes pressure problems. The checks here are different — fittings, manifolds and the water heater — rather than the pipes themselves.

Across inland California, hard water is the thread running through all three eras: it scales galvanised lines, clogs copper fittings and settles in PEX manifolds alike. Whatever the pipe material, a water heater that is flushed annually and an anode rod that is replaced on schedule will outlast one that is never serviced.

Plumbing problems common across California

Whatever the town, California plumbing fails in familiar ways. In Corona, these five are the ones to watch for.

  • Washing machine hoses — rubber supply hoses age and burst without warning, and they are one of the most common causes of indoor flooding.
