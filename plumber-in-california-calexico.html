What your home’s age means

What Calexico’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in Calexico all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in Calexico usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Homes built before about 1960 usually have galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains. Galvanised steel rusts from the inside, so the bore narrows over decades: pressure falls, water runs brown after a holiday away, and eventually a pinhole leak appears. Cast-iron drains scale and sag in the same way. A home of this age in Calexico is worth a proper look at the pipework, even if nothing has failed yet — the first signs are easy to miss.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Calexico. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

PEX and CPVC define homes built from the 1990s onward: flexible pipe that resists freezing and scale, and installs quickly. The weak points moved, they did not vanish — fittings, manifolds and the quality of the installation now decide whether the system is trouble-free. Exposed runs still need protection from sun, and a bad manifold will show up as pressure problems in one part of the house and not another.

In the desert, the sun is the factor no era escapes: ultraviolet light makes exposed plastic brittle, and summer heat expands every joint. Outdoor runs need UV-rated material and shading whatever the house was built of, and hard water scales heaters and fixtures in homes of every age.

In depth

Common plumbing problems in Calexico, in depth

The four problems above are the ones we see most often in Calexico. Below is how to stop each of them coming back, plus two more faults that the California desert conditions around Calexico produce regularly, covered in full.

01

Clogged drains due to sediment buildup

How to prevent it: Do not keep clearing the same drain: a fault that returns is telling you the cause is still there, and a camera inspection will find it.

02

Leaky faucets and fixtures

How to prevent it: Look for the early signs — a drip that will not stop, a stain on the wall, a warm patch on the floor — and act on them. A leak caught early is a washer; the same leak ignored for a season is a ruined cabinet.

03

Running toilets

How to prevent it: A toilet that rocks, even slightly, has a failed seal underneath. Do not wait for the leak to show on the ceiling below — a new wax ring is a small job now and a floor repair later.

04

Water heater failures

How to prevent it: Flush the tank once a year and replace the anode rod on schedule. If the hot water runs out faster than it used to, book the flush before the heater gives up entirely.

05

Sewer odours from dried traps

Why it happens: Traps dry out in unused bathrooms and guest rooms, and heat speeds it up — once the water seal is gone, sewer smells come straight up the drain.

How we fix it: We run water through every trap, check the venting, and find any trap that is not holding its seal. A trap that keeps drying out usually has a venting fault behind it.

How to prevent it: Run water in every sink, shower and bath at least once a month, including the ones nobody uses. A trap that stays full is a trap that keeps smells out.

06

Leaking washing-machine hoses baked by heat

Why it happens: Heat bakes rubber supply hoses until they go brittle, and a hose that bursts can flood a room in minutes — usually while nobody is home.

How we fix it: We replace rubber hoses with braided steel, fit shut-offs, and check the connections. In desert heat, braided hoses last far longer than rubber.

How to prevent it: Check hoses once a year for bulges, cracks or stiffness, and turn the supply off when you go away for more than a few days.

Water and weather in Calexico: what it does to plumbing

Plumbing fails for local reasons. In Calexico the local reasons are extreme summer heat and hard, mineral-rich water, plus extremely hot summers and mild winters — and understanding them is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that comes back next season.

Heat is the plumbing problem in Calexico. Summer temperatures expand exposed supply lines, bake hoses and irrigation connections, and degrade plastic pipe and fittings left in direct sun — ultraviolet light makes exposed PVC and PEX brittle over time, and a fitting that was fine in spring can crack by September. Hard water is the desert's slow cost. Descaling the fixtures restores flow in minutes, and flushing the heater once a year is what keeps it alive. If the hot water runs out faster than it used to, scale is the first thing to check.

Flash flooding is the desert's acute problem. Monsoon storms can dump a season's rain in an afternoon, and sandy soils wash debris into every low point, blocking drains that were fine all year. Keeping gutters and surface drains clear before storm season, and making sure water runs away from the house, is the whole job — and it is worth doing before the first storm, not after.

Plumbing problems common in the California desert

Around Calexico, the desert sets the plumbing agenda: extreme heat by day, cold nights, and some of the hardest water in the state.

  • Sand in outdoor drains — wind-blown sand settles in drains, gullies and condensate lines until a storm backs water up.
  • Irrigation line failures — garden watering systems run hard all summer, and a cracked line can waste a large volume before anyone notices.
