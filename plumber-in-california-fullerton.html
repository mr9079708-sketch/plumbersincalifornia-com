What your home’s age means

What Fullerton’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in Fullerton all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in Fullerton usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Before 1960, the standard was galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both are now past their best in Fullerton. The steel rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure falls and the water runs brown; the cast iron scales and sags until drains block more often. Test the lines before anything fails — a pressure and flow test, plus a camera look at the drains, tells you what is urgent and what can wait.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

Copper and plastic define the 1960s to 1980s: copper supply lines, ABS or PVC drains. It was a good era for plumbing, and most of it is still sound — but copper can develop pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains are now old enough for joints and fittings to fail. The checks here are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the drains that serve the busiest bathrooms.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

Homes built from the 1990s onward usually have PEX or CPVC supply lines — flexible, resistant to freezing and scale, and quick to install. They are not trouble-free: fittings matter, ultraviolet light degrades exposed runs, and a bad manifold installation causes pressure problems. The checks here are different — fittings, manifolds and the water heater — rather than the pipes themselves.

On the coast, the outside of the house matters as much as the inside: salt air corrodes exposed fittings and vents, damp sea air keeps crawl spaces moist, and winter storms test gutters and drains. A home here needs its outdoor pipework looked over once a year, whatever era it was built in.

In depth

Common plumbing problems in Fullerton, in depth

The four problems above are the ones we see most often in Fullerton. Below is what prevents each of them, plus two more faults that the California coast conditions around Fullerton produce regularly, covered in full.

01

Clogged drains due to tree roots

How to prevent it: Find the main shut-off valve before you need it, and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is. In a leak, the first minute matters more than the first hour.

02

Leaky faucets and fixtures

How to prevent it: Replace supply lines and washers before they fail: a braided supply line that is ten years old is a burst waiting to happen, and it is a small job to change.

03

Water heater failures

How to prevent it: Flush the tank once a year and replace the anode rod on schedule. If the hot water runs out faster than it used to, book the flush before the heater gives up entirely.

04

Running toilets

How to prevent it: Do not keep jiggling the handle or topping up the cistern: a toilet that runs is usually a worn flapper or a mis-set fill valve, both cheap fixes if they are caught early.

05

Seized shut-off valves

Why it happens: Stopcocks and shut-off valves seize from disuse and corrosion, and the failure only shows up in an emergency, when the water cannot be turned off.

How we fix it: We free the valve where possible and replace it with a lever valve where it has seized solid, so the water can be shut off in seconds.

How to prevent it: Work every shut-off valve in the house twice a year — kitchen, bathroom, water heater, and the main. A valve that moves freely is the cheapest insurance in plumbing.

06

Noisy pipes and water hammer

Why it happens: A bang when a tap shuts, or a rattle when the washing machine fills, usually means loose clips, high pressure, or air in the lines — and it gets worse, not better, if it is ignored.

How we fix it: We secure the loose runs, check the pressure, and fit a water hammer arrestor where the bang is worst. The fix is usually small; the damage from ignoring it is not.

How to prevent it: Note when the noise started and which tap causes it — that history tells the technician where to look, and whether pressure or a loose clip is the cause.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Fullerton

Winter

Tip: Insulate exposed pipes before the cold arrives, and keep the house heated — even rooms that are not used much.

Water and weather in Fullerton

Water and weather in Fullerton: what it does to plumbing

Plumbing fails for local reasons. In Fullerton the local reasons are salt air and damp sea air, plus mild, damp winters and long dry summers — and understanding them is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that comes back next season.

The sea sets the terms for plumbing in Fullerton. Salt air is the slow problem: it corrodes exposed metal — outdoor taps, hose bibs, pipe clips, water-heater vents and the fittings on pool and irrigation equipment — faster than the same parts would wear inland. The fix is unglamorous but effective: brass or stainless fittings outside, pipework clipped with coated fixings, and a look over the exposed runs once a year. Catching surface rust early is a ten-minute job; leaving it is how a hose bib snaps off in your hand or a vent collar rusts through.

Storms are the coast's acute problem, to salt air's chronic one. A winter storm can dump a lot of rain in a short burst, and the damage is done by water with nowhere to go: a blocked gutter, a silted surface drain, a downspout that ends against the wall. Clearing gutters before the rainy season and checking that water runs away from the house is the whole job.

The third coastal factor is the one people notice least: the air gets into the house, not just the garden. Damp sea air keeps the shaded side of a home moist, which is hard on crawl spaces, on the pipework under the house, and on any fitting that never quite dries. Ventilating the crawl space and lagging cold-water lines so they do not sweat will keep the damp from becoming damage — and a look under the house once a year is worth doing.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common on the California coast

Coastal homes face a particular mix of plumbing trouble. Salt, sand and damp work on pipework year-round, and these are the faults Fullerton residents call about most.

  • Leaks that hide in damp air — a slow drip under a coastal home can go unnoticed for months because everything down there is already damp.
  • Low water pressure from corroded lines — internal rust narrows old galvanised pipes until showers run weak and taps slow to a trickle.
  • Gutters and downspouts working overtime — sea storms dump a lot of water fast, and a blocked gutter sends it straight down the wall and into the foundations.
  • Outdoor showers and pool equipment — coastal homes often have outdoor showers and pool plumbing, and their exposed fittings corrode faster than indoor ones.
  • Irrigation and garden lines — watering systems run all year near the coast, and a cracked line or backflow fault can waste water for weeks before anyone notices.

Most of these are cheap to prevent and expensive to ignore — a yearly check of outdoor pipework and drains catches the majority before they fail.
