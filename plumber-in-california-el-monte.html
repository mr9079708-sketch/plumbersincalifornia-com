What your home’s age means

What El Monte’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in El Monte all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in El Monte usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

A pre-1960 home in El Monte usually runs on galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains, and both wear from the inside out. The supply lines rust and narrow, so pressure falls and water runs brown after the house has stood empty; the drains scale and sag, so they block more often and clear more slowly. The work on a home of this age is usually staged: test the lines, replace the worst run first, and plan the rest over a year or two rather than all at once.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in El Monte. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

Homes built from the 1990s onward usually have PEX or CPVC supply lines — flexible, resistant to freezing and scale, and quick to install. They are not trouble-free: fittings matter, ultraviolet light degrades exposed runs, and a bad manifold installation causes pressure problems. The checks here are different — fittings, manifolds and the water heater — rather than the pipes themselves.

On the coast, the outside of the house matters as much as the inside: salt air corrodes exposed fittings and vents, damp sea air keeps crawl spaces moist, and winter storms test gutters and drains. A home here needs its outdoor pipework looked over once a year, whatever era it was built in.

Common plumbing problems in El Monte, in depth

The four problems above are the ones we see most often in El Monte. Below is how to stop each of them coming back, plus two more faults that the California coast conditions around El Monte produce regularly, covered in full.

01

Preventing frequent pipe bursts during winter

How to prevent it: Replace supply lines and washers before they fail: a braided supply line that is ten years old is a burst waiting to happen, and it is a small job to change.

02

Preventing clogged drains due to tree roots

How to prevent it: Find the main shut-off valve before you need it, and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is. In a leak, the first minute matters more than the first hour.

03

Preventing leaky faucets and fixtures

How to prevent it: Replace supply lines and washers before they fail: a braided supply line that is ten years old is a burst waiting to happen, and it is a small job to change.

04

Preventing sewer line backups

How to prevent it: Have the sewer line camera-inspected every couple of years — roots, cracks and sagging sections show up long before they back up — and keep wipes and grease out of the drains.

05

Seized shut-off valves

Why it happens: Stopcocks and shut-off valves seize from disuse and corrosion, and the failure only shows up in an emergency, when the water cannot be turned off.

How we fix it: We free the valve where possible and replace it with a lever valve where it has seized solid, so the water can be shut off in seconds.

How to prevent it: Work every shut-off valve in the house twice a year — kitchen, bathroom, water heater, and the main. A valve that moves freely is the cheapest insurance in plumbing.

06

Noisy pipes and water hammer

Why it happens: A bang when a tap shuts, or a rattle when the washing machine fills, usually means loose clips, high pressure, or air in the lines — and it gets worse, not better, if it is ignored.

How we fix it: We secure the loose runs, check the pressure, and fit a water hammer arrestor where the bang is worst. The fix is usually small; the damage from ignoring it is not.

How to prevent it: Note when the noise started and which tap causes it — that history tells the technician where to look, and whether pressure or a loose clip is the cause.

Water and weather in El Monte

Water and weather in El Monte: what it does to plumbing

Plumbing fails for local reasons. In El Monte the local reasons are salt air and damp sea air, plus mild, damp winters and long dry summers — and understanding them is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that comes back next season.

Salt air does its damage slowly, which is why it is easy to ignore. A hose bib that worked fine last summer seizes this one; a vent collar that looked sound rusts through at the joint. The parts to watch are the ones outside: taps, clips, brackets, the water-heater vent and any exposed pipework. Brass or stainless fittings, coated fixings and a yearly look-over are the whole defence — and the look-over costs nothing if it is done during another visit.

Damp is the second coastal factor. Sea air keeps crawl spaces and the shaded side of a house moist for much of the year, and winter storms can dump a lot of rain in a short burst. That combination is hard on drains and gutters: a blocked gutter sends water down the wall and into the foundations, and a slow drain turns into a backup when the storm drain outside is already full. Clearing gutters before the rainy season and keeping surface drains running away from the house prevents most of it.

The third coastal factor is the one people notice least: the air gets into the house, not just the garden. Damp sea air keeps the shaded side of a home moist, which is hard on crawl spaces, on the pipework under the house, and on any fitting that never quite dries. Ventilating the crawl space and lagging cold-water lines so they do not sweat will keep the damp from becoming damage — and a look under the house once a year is worth doing.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common on the California coast

Living near the sea is easy on people and hard on plumbing. These five problems account for most coastal call-outs in places like El Monte.

  • Gutters and downspouts working overtime — sea storms dump a lot of water fast, and a blocked gutter sends it straight down the wall and into the foundations.
  • Outdoor showers and pool equipment — coastal homes often have outdoor showers and pool plumbing, and their exposed fittings corrode faster than indoor ones.
  • Irrigation and garden lines — watering systems run all year near the coast, and a cracked line or backflow fault can waste water for weeks before anyone notices.
  • Corroded outdoor taps and fittings — salt air eats unprotected metal, so exterior pipework, hose bibs and clips rust faster than the same parts inland.
  • Sand and grit in outdoor drains — wind-blown sand settles in gullies and yard drains, slowing them until a heavy rain backs water up.

Most of these are cheap to prevent and expensive to ignore — a yearly check of outdoor pipework and drains catches the majority before they fail.
