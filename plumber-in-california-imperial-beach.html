Know your home

Housing in Imperial Beach: what your pipes are made of — and why it matters

Houses in Imperial Beach were not all plumbed the same way. Four eras of pipework are still in daily use across the region, and each one ages differently. Here is how to read your own home.

Before the 1960s: galvanised steel and cast iron

Pre-1960s homes in Imperial Beach were plumbed in galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both materials age the same way — from the inside. Corrosion narrows galvanised pipe until pressure drops and water discolours; cast iron scales, cracks and invites root intrusion. Patching works for a while, but when the whole house has lost pressure, a planned repipe in copper or PEX is the durable answer.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

Homes built from the 1960s onwards in Imperial Beach usually run copper supply lines with ABS or PVC drains. Copper lasts well, but it is not immortal: aggressive water can cause pinhole leaks, and soldered joints age. The drains from this era are generally sound, though the sewer lateral — the run from the house to the street — is often clay or cast iron even when the house itself is copper, and that is where roots get in. A camera inspection settles the question in an hour.

1990s to 2000s: copper and early PEX

The 1990s and 2000s brought early PEX alongside a lot more copper, with PVC drains throughout. PEX from this period resists scale well, which suits Imperial Beach, but early fittings and manifolds deserve inspection — a manifold weeping slowly behind a panel is easy to miss. Heaters from this era are also reaching the end of their working lives, so a heater check is worth doing even while the hot water still runs.

2010s onwards: PEX and CPVC

Newer building in Imperial Beach means PEX or CPVC, usually from a manifold so each fixture can be shut off separately. Failures are uncommon and usually sit at a fitting or valve rather than in a run of pipe. Tankless water heaters are frequent in this era and need regular descaling; where the water is hard, that schedule is not optional.

How to tell what you have: look at an exposed pipe near the water heater or under a sink. Galvanised steel is dull grey, magnetic and joined with threads; copper is reddish-brown with soldered joints; PEX is flexible plastic, often red for hot and blue for cold; CPVC is rigid cream-coloured plastic.

What we check on a visit in Imperial Beach: the pipe material and its condition, water pressure at more than one tap, the water heater's age and anode, and every visible joint for weeping. The report is plain: what is fine, what needs watching, and what should be planned before it fails.

Plumbing problems common on the California coast

Five faults, year after year: that is the pattern in Imperial Beach, and it follows directly from the California coast. The short list is here; the in-depth version, with causes, fixes and prevention, is further down the page.

  • Storm drains overwhelmed by heavy rain — short, sharp downpours can flood low spots when gutters and surface drains are even partly blocked.
  • Rust on exposed pipework — within a few miles of the shore, bare metal fittings can show rust within a year or two of being fitted.
  • Outdoor drains choked with sand — coastal grit builds up in yard drains and condensate lines, and the first sign is usually water pooling after rain.
  • Leaks that hide in damp air — a slow drip under a coastal home can go unnoticed for months because everything down there is already damp.
  • Low water pressure from corroded lines — internal rust narrows old galvanised pipes until showers run weak and taps slow to a trickle.

Most of these are cheap to prevent and expensive to ignore — a yearly check of outdoor pipework and drains catches the majority before they fail.
