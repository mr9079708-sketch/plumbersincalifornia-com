What your home’s age means

What Cupertino’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in Cupertino all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in Cupertino usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Before 1960, the standard was galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both are now past their best in Cupertino. The steel rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure falls and the water runs brown; the cast iron scales and sags until drains block more often. Test the lines before anything fails — a pressure and flow test, plus a camera look at the drains, tells you what is urgent and what can wait.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

Copper and plastic define the 1960s to 1980s: copper supply lines, ABS or PVC drains. It was a good era for plumbing, and most of it is still sound — but copper can develop pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains are now old enough for joints and fittings to fail. The checks here are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the drains that serve the busiest bathrooms.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

From the 1990s onward, PEX and CPVC took over: flexible, freeze-resistant, quick to install. The failure points moved to the fittings and the manifold, and to exposed runs left in the sun. A home of this era in Cupertino rarely needs repiping — it needs its fittings, its manifold and its water heater checked, which is a smaller job and a shorter visit.

In the Bay Area, the ground is the factor no era escapes: clay soils swell and shrink with the seasons, working joints open and bellying buried laterals. Whatever the supply pipes are made of, a camera inspection of the lateral is worth doing on any older home here.

Common plumbing problems in Cupertino, in depth

The four problems above are the ones we see most often in Cupertino. Below is what prevents each of them, plus two more faults that the Bay Area conditions around Cupertino produce regularly, covered in full.

01

Clogged drains due to tree roots

How to prevent it: Do not keep clearing the same drain: a fault that returns is telling you the cause is still there, and a camera inspection will find it.

02

Leaky pipes in older homes

How to prevent it: Replace supply lines and washers before they fail: a braided supply line that is ten years old is a burst waiting to happen, and it is a small job to change.

03

Water heater failures

How to prevent it: A maintenance visit once a year covers the heater: the tank, the anode rod, the thermostat and the venting, checked before the season that stresses it most.

04

Sewer line backups

How to prevent it: Prevention is a yearly look-over: the shut-off valve, the water heater, the visible pipework and every tap and toilet, checked before the season that stresses them most.

05

Slab leaks under concrete floors

Why it happens: Many Bay Area homes sit on concrete slabs, and a pinhole leak in a buried hot-water line shows up as a warm patch on the floor, a faint sound of running water, or a water bill that climbs for no reason.

How we fix it: We locate the leak with acoustic and electronic detection, then reroute the line overhead or make a spot repair — whichever does less damage to the home.

How to prevent it: Know your normal water bill. A sudden rise with no change in use is the earliest sign of a slab leak, and catching it early is the difference between a reroute and a ruined floor.

06

Leaking washing-machine and dishwasher hoses

Why it happens: Rubber supply hoses perish with age and heat, and when one bursts it can flood a room in minutes — usually while nobody is home.

How we fix it: We replace rubber hoses with braided steel, fit shut-offs, and check the connections. A burst hose is one of the most expensive small failures in a home.

How to prevent it: Check hoses once a year for bulges, cracks or rust at the fittings, and turn the supply off when you go away for more than a few days.

Plumbing problems common in the Bay Area

In the Bay Area, the age of the housing stock does most of the damage. These are the five faults Cupertino homeowners meet most often.

  • Damp winters and fog — cool, moist air keeps crawl spaces damp for months, hiding slow leaks and corroding exposed fittings.
