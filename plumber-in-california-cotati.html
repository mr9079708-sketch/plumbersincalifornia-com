What your home’s age means

What Cotati’s homes mean for your plumbing

The age of a house tells you more about its plumbing than almost anything else. A home built before 1960, one built in the 1970s, and one built last year have different pipes, different failure points and different jobs waiting for them — and in Cotati all three sit on the same streets. This is what the age of a home in Cotati usually means for the plumbing inside it.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Homes built before about 1960 usually have galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains. Galvanised steel rusts from the inside, so the bore narrows over decades: pressure falls, water runs brown after a holiday away, and eventually a pinhole leak appears. Cast-iron drains scale and sag in the same way. A home of this age in Cotati is worth a proper look at the pipework, even if nothing has failed yet — the first signs are easy to miss.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Cotati. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

PEX and CPVC define homes built from the 1990s onward: flexible pipe that resists freezing and scale, and installs quickly. The weak points moved, they did not vanish — fittings, manifolds and the quality of the installation now decide whether the system is trouble-free. Exposed runs still need protection from sun, and a bad manifold will show up as pressure problems in one part of the house and not another.

In the Bay Area, the ground adds a factor no era escapes: expansive clay soils swell in winter and shrink in summer, working joints open and bellying buried sewer laterals. A camera inspection of the lateral is worth doing on any older home here, whatever the supply pipes are made of.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common in the Bay Area

Bay Area plumbing has a character of its own: old pipes, hills, big trees and earthquakes. These are the problems that come up again and again in Cotati.

  • Ageing galvanised pipes — many homes still have original steel supply lines that corrode from the inside, cutting pressure and tinting the water brown.
  • Tree roots in sewer laterals — mature street trees send roots into old clay and cast-iron joints, and the first sign is slow drains in several rooms at once.
  • Hillside drain runs — long laterals on sloped lots are harder to clear and more prone to sagging sections where waste collects.
  • Seismic vulnerability — rigid gas and water connections can fail in a quake, which is why flexible connectors and shut-off valves are standard precautions here.
  • Damp winters and fog — cool, moist air keeps crawl spaces damp for months, hiding slow leaks and corroding exposed fittings.

If the house predates the 1970s, assume the pipes deserve a look — the inspection is cheap and the surprises it prevents are not.
