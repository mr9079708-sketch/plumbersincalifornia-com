What your home’s age means

What Bayside’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Bayside can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Homes built before about 1960 usually have galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains. Galvanised steel rusts from the inside, so the bore narrows over decades: pressure falls, water runs brown after a holiday away, and eventually a pinhole leak appears. Cast-iron drains scale and sag in the same way. A home of this age in Bayside is worth a proper look at the pipework, even if nothing has failed yet — the first signs are easy to miss.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Bayside. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

PEX and CPVC define homes built from the 1990s onward: flexible pipe that resists freezing and scale, and installs quickly. The weak points moved, they did not vanish — fittings, manifolds and the quality of the installation now decide whether the system is trouble-free. Exposed runs still need protection from sun, and a bad manifold will show up as pressure problems in one part of the house and not another.

On the North Coast, damp is the thread through every era: months of wet weather keep crawl spaces moist and corrode exposed pipework, whatever it is made of. Ventilation under the house, clear gutters and lagged cold-water lines matter as much here as the pipe material in the walls.

Common plumbing problems in Bayside, in depth

The four problems above are the ones we see most often in Bayside. Below is what prevents each of them, plus two more faults that the North Coast conditions around Bayside produce regularly, covered in full.

01

Clogged drains due to debris

How to prevent it: Keep the area around the fault clear and dry, and note when the problem started — that history helps the technician find the cause faster.

02

Leaky faucets and fixtures

How to prevent it: Check the places leaks start — under sinks, behind toilets, around the water heater — once a year, and fix a weeping joint before it becomes a stain on the ceiling below.

03

Running toilets

How to prevent it: Check the toilet once a year — the flapper, the fill valve, the seal at the base. A rocking toilet is a failed wax ring quietly rotting the floor underneath.

04

Water heater failures

How to prevent it: Flush the tank once a year and replace the anode rod on schedule. If the hot water runs out faster than it used to, book the flush before the heater gives up entirely.

05

Noisy pipes and water hammer

Why it happens: A bang when a tap shuts, or a rattle when the washing machine fills, usually means loose clips, high pressure, or air in the lines — and it gets worse, not better, if it is ignored.

How we fix it: We secure the loose runs, check the pressure, and fit a water hammer arrestor where the bang is worst. The fix is usually small; the damage from ignoring it is not.

How to prevent it: Note when the noise started and which tap causes it — that history tells the technician where to look, and whether pressure or a loose clip is the cause.

06

Seized shut-off valves

Why it happens: Stopcocks and shut-off valves seize from disuse and corrosion, and the failure only shows up in an emergency, when the water cannot be turned off.

How we fix it: We free the valve where possible and replace it with a lever valve where it has seized solid, so the water can be shut off in seconds.

How to prevent it: Work every shut-off valve in the house twice a year — kitchen, bathroom, water heater, and the main. A valve that moves freely is the cheapest insurance in plumbing.

Trees are the North Coast's other plumbing factor. Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common on the North Coast

The North Coast is wet, wooded and old — and its plumbing shows it. These are the recurring problems in Bayside.

  • Persistent damp corroding fittings — crawl spaces stay moist for months, so exposed fittings rust and slow leaks go unnoticed.
  • Tree roots in sewer lines — redwood and fir roots find old clay and cast-iron joints, and slow drains across the house are the giveaway.
  • Mould from hidden leaks — damp air plus an unnoticed drip is the recipe for mould in crawl spaces and behind walls.
  • Ageing cast-iron and clay sewers — older North Coast homes often still run on original sewer pipe that cracks with ground movement.
  • Gutters and downspouts giving up — months of heavy rain expose every weak joint, and a failed gutter sends water straight into the foundations.

Clear the gutters before the rains, camera the sewer line if drains slow across the house, and check the crawl space yearly — the North Coast routine.
