Understanding kitchen plumbing in California

Kitchen plumbing covers the pipes, fixtures and appliances in the room that is used hardest: the faucet, the sink, the drain and the water heater. It means fixing what is broken, a leaking faucet, a clogged sink, a water heater that cannot keep up, and installing new fixtures properly, so the kitchen is back in use as quickly as possible.

  • ✓ Enhanced home value with a sound kitchen
  • ✓ Improved water efficiency in daily use
  • ✓ Preventative maintenance that stops bigger jobs
  • ✓ Code compliance for installations and repairs

Kitchen plumbing ages faster than plumbing anywhere else in the home, because it is used more. The faucet is turned on and off dozens of times a day, the sink takes grease and food every day, and the pipes under the sink sit in a damp cabinet where a small leak can go unnoticed for weeks. That is why a kitchen visit checks the cabinet and the floor beneath the sink as well as the visible fittings, and why the final test is done with the water running: a kitchen repair is only finished when the sink drains properly, the faucet does not drip, and the cabinet underneath is dry.

Typical scenarios

Typical kitchen plumbing problems we are called for

The kitchen asks more of its plumbing than any other room, and the problems it develops are usually the same few. If yours looks like one of these, the cause is usually the same.

A faucet that drips into the cabinet below

A kitchen faucet that drips is annoying, but the drip you cannot see is the one that does the damage. A worn washer or cartridge can let water seep into the cabinet below, where it damages the wood and can sit for weeks before anyone notices. The visit starts with checking the faucet and the cabinet underneath, and the fix is usually replacing the worn part and resealing the fitting, then checking with the water running to make sure the cabinet stays dry.

A sink that backs up after cooking

A kitchen sink that backs up, especially after cooking, is usually grease. Grease goes down the drain as a liquid and sets as a coating inside the pipe, and food particles stick to it. The sink drains a little slower each week until it backs up. The fix is drain cleaning, usually with a snake for a local blockage or jetting where the pipe is coated, followed by advice on keeping grease out of the sink in future.

Hot water that runs out during cooking and cleaning

A kitchen that runs out of hot water during cooking and cleaning is often a water heater problem rather than a kitchen problem. The technician checks the water heater as part of the visit: whether it is the right size for the household, whether a part has failed, and whether sediment has built up inside the tank. The answer may be a water heater repair, or it may be that the kitchen needs a different arrangement, and the estimate will set out the options honestly.

Questions about kitchen plumbing problems

You should call a kitchen plumbing professional when you notice persistent leaks, low water pressure, or if you are planning a kitchen remodel. A leak that keeps coming back, a faucet that loses pressure, or a drain that keeps clogging are all signs that the problem is beyond a quick fix.

Common kitchen plumbing issues include clogged drains and leaky faucets, along with low water pressure and a water heater that cannot keep up with cooking and cleaning. Addressing these promptly can prevent more extensive damage. In the kitchen itself, the most frequent problems are grease-blocked drains and leaking faucets, both of which get worse the longer they are left.

Questions about sinks, faucets and drains

A smell from a kitchen sink usually means food and grease are sitting in the drain or in the trap, even if the water still drains. Grease coats the inside of the pipe, food particles stick to it, and the buildup gives off a smell. Running more water does not remove it. The fix is a proper drain cleaning, which removes the buildup from the pipe walls, and a check of the trap, which holds water to block smells from coming up from the drain. If the trap has dried out or is damaged, smells will come through even when the drain is clear. After the clean, keeping grease out of the sink is what stops the smell coming back.

Most leaking kitchen faucets should be repaired, because the cause is usually one worn part: a washer, a cartridge, or a seal. Replacing that part is a short job and sits at the lower end of the $150 to $850 range. A replacement makes sense when the faucet itself is corroded or damaged, when parts are no longer available for it, or when the faucet is old and has already been repaired more than once. The estimate will say which one your job is, and whether the price includes the new faucet itself or only the labour to fit it.

Grease is the main one: it goes down as a liquid and sets as a coating inside the pipe, and food sticks to it. Coffee grounds, starchy foods such as rice and pasta, and fibrous food waste also cause blockages, because they swell or tangle inside the pipe. Even with a garbage disposal, these should not go down the sink in quantity. The simplest habit is to scrape plates into the bin and wipe grease out of pans before washing them. A drain cleaned once a year, and grease kept out of the sink, is what keeps a kitchen drain clear.
