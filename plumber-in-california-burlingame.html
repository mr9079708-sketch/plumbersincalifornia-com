What your home’s age means

What Burlingame’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Burlingame can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

Before 1960, the standard was galvanised steel for supply and cast iron for drains, and both are now past their best in Burlingame. The steel rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure falls and the water runs brown; the cast iron scales and sags until drains block more often. Test the lines before anything fails — a pressure and flow test, plus a camera look at the drains, tells you what is urgent and what can wait.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Burlingame. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

PEX and CPVC define homes built from the 1990s onward: flexible pipe that resists freezing and scale, and installs quickly. The weak points moved, they did not vanish — fittings, manifolds and the quality of the installation now decide whether the system is trouble-free. Exposed runs still need protection from sun, and a bad manifold will show up as pressure problems in one part of the house and not another.

In the Bay Area, the ground adds a factor no era escapes: expansive clay soils swell in winter and shrink in summer, working joints open and bellying buried sewer laterals. A camera inspection of the lateral is worth doing on any older home here, whatever the supply pipes are made of.

How to prevent it: If a drain slows every winter or a backup clears and returns, do not keep clearing it: get the camera in. Clearing without repairing the entry point only buys a few months.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Burlingame

Burlingame's weather shapes its plumbing across the year. The notes below cover what each season usually brings — and what to check before it arrives.

Spring

Spring is when winter's damage shows up: gutters full of debris, drains that slowed over the cold months, and the first heavy rain testing all of it at once.

Tip: Clear the gutters and check the drains before the spring rain arrives — a slow drain in March is a backup in April.

Summer

Summer is the season for outdoor plumbing: hoses, irrigation and the lines that sit in the sun all get used hard, and heat expands every joint in the system.

Tip: Check outdoor taps and hose connections in early summer, and watch the water bill — a rise with no change in use usually means a leak somewhere.

Fall

Fall is a time for preparing plumbing for winter, including checking for leaks and insulating pipes.

Tip: Consider scheduling a maintenance check to ensure your plumbing is ready for cooler temperatures.

Winter

Winter is when the cold tests the plumbing: a cold snap can freeze an exposed pipe, a scaled heater gives up under the extra load, and the first storm tests every drain.

Tip: Insulate exposed pipes before the cold arrives, and keep the house heated — even rooms that are not used much.

Water and weather in Burlingame

Water and weather in Burlingame: what it does to plumbing

Plumbing fails for local reasons. In Burlingame the local reasons are older housing stock, clay soils and damp fog, plus cool, foggy summers and wet winters — and understanding them is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that comes back next season.

The Bay Area's pipes are older than its houses look. Galvanised supply lines narrow with rust over decades, cast-iron drains scale and sag, and the signs creep up slowly: a shower that has lost pressure over the years, water that runs brown after a holiday away. A pressure and flow test tells you in one visit how much life the lines have left — and whether a single run or a staged repipe is the honest answer.

The ground here moves with the seasons. Expansive clay soils swell in the wet winter and shrink in the dry summer, and that movement works on everything buried: sewer laterals belly and joints open, water lines shift, and tree roots find the gaps. A camera inspection of the lateral is the only way to know what is happening underground — and it is worth doing before buying an older home in Burlingame, or after any backup that clears and then returns.

Fog is the Bay Area's damp problem. It keeps the shaded side of a house moist most of the year, which is hard on crawl spaces, exposed pipework and outdoor fittings — and it never quite dries out. Ventilating the crawl space, lagging cold-water lines so they do not sweat, and looking over outdoor taps once a year will keep the damp from becoming damage.

Local problem patterns

Plumbing problems common in the Bay Area

Bay Area plumbing has a character of its own: old pipes, hills, big trees and earthquakes. These are the problems that come up again and again in Burlingame.

  • Tree roots in sewer laterals — mature street trees send roots into old clay and cast-iron joints, and the first sign is slow drains in several rooms at once.
  • Hillside drain runs — long laterals on sloped lots are harder to clear and more prone to sagging sections where waste collects.
  • Seismic vulnerability — rigid gas and water connections can fail in a quake, which is why flexible connectors and shut-off valves are standard precautions here.
  • Damp winters and fog — cool, moist air keeps crawl spaces damp for months, hiding slow leaks and corroding exposed fittings.
  • Discoloured water from old pipes — rust inside ageing supply lines shows up first as brown water from a tap that has stood overnight.

If the house predates the 1970s, assume the pipes deserve a look — the inspection is cheap and the surprises it prevents are not.
