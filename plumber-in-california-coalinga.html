What your home’s age means

What Coalinga’s homes mean for your plumbing

Two houses on the same street in Coalinga can have completely different plumbing, because the year they were built decides what is in the walls. Knowing what era a home belongs to tells you what to check, what is likely to fail first, and whether a repair or a replacement is the honest answer.

Built before 1960: galvanised steel and cast iron

A pre-1960 home in Coalinga usually runs on galvanised steel supply pipes and cast-iron drains, and both wear from the inside out. The supply lines rust and narrow, so pressure falls and water runs brown after the house has stood empty; the drains scale and sag, so they block more often and clear more slowly. The work on a home of this age is usually staged: test the lines, replace the worst run first, and plan the rest over a year or two rather than all at once.

1960s to 1980s: copper supply, plastic drains

The 1960s to 1980s brought copper supply lines and plastic drains, and most of that work is still sound in Coalinga. Copper's weakness is pinhole leaks where the water chemistry is aggressive, and the drains' weakness is age — joints and fittings from this era are now old enough to fail. The checks are targeted: pressure, visible joints, and the busiest bathrooms first.

1990s to today: PEX and CPVC

From the 1990s onward, PEX and CPVC took over: flexible, freeze-resistant, quick to install. The failure points moved to the fittings and the manifold, and to exposed runs left in the sun. A home of this era in Coalinga rarely needs repiping — it needs its fittings, its manifold and its water heater checked, which is a smaller job and a shorter visit.

In the Valley, hard water is the thread running through all three eras: it scales galvanised lines, clogs copper fittings and settles in PEX manifolds alike. Whatever the pipe material, a water heater that is flushed annually and an anode rod that is replaced on schedule will outlast one that is never serviced.
